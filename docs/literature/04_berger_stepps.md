# Berger — Contagious (STEPPS) and Berger & Milkman (2012)

**Claim.** Social currency, triggers, emotion, public visibility, practical value and stories can increase the likelihood of word of mouth (Berger 2013, a trade book synthesising research). Berger & Milkman (2012) found that New York Times articles evoking high-arousal emotions were more likely to be highly e-mailed.

**In DSDVC.** `content/stepps.py` (measurement with evidence/confidence), `content/stepps_models.py` (additive vs multiplicative vs interaction compared out of sample), STEPPS appeal in the simulator, Copilot variants.

**Not established.** A functional form, weights, or a guarantee of virality. Berger stresses that STEPPS raises the odds of sharing; DSDVC therefore treats STEPPS as a hypothesis tested against observed sharing.

## References
- Berger, J. (2013). Contagious: Why Things Catch On. Simon & Schuster.
- Berger, J., & Milkman, K. L. (2012). What Makes Online Content Viral? Journal of Marketing Research, 49(2), 192-205.
